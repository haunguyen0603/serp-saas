## Start with the client's reporting question

An agency rank report should make a decision easier. Before importing keywords, agree on what the client needs to understand: visibility for a service area, progress after a content update, differences between desktop and mobile, or the sites appearing beside them in search.

Write a short tracking brief for each client. Include the site, market, language, device, keyword scope and review cadence. Keep this brief with your own account notes so another review does not silently change the meaning of the dataset.

If you are setting up tracking for the first time, our [Google ranking baseline guide](/blog/how-to-track-google-rankings) explains the measurement fundamentals. This guide focuses on organizing and communicating that work across clients.

For the product capabilities supporting this process, see [SERP SaaS for agency rank tracking](/serp-tracking-for-agencies): multiple client Projects in one account, keyword organization, competitor comparisons and report exports.

## Keep client boundaries clear

### Use Projects for distinct search environments

A Project should have an understandable purpose and a consistent environment. If a client operates in two markets, avoid blending those markets into one summary. Name Projects so the client, market and device are easy to identify in a list.

For example, an internal naming convention might be `Client — UK — English — Desktop`. A corresponding mobile dataset should remain separately labeled. When reviewing limits, remember that SERP SaaS has separate Desktop and Mobile Plans.

### Reuse a Tracked Site intentionally

Connect the correct Tracked Site to the Project and review its aliases. A reusable site identity can keep matching consistent across Projects, but that also means an alias change deserves care. Current active aliases affect matching against stored SERP results.

Keep a dated note explaining additions such as a legitimate subdomain or a domain move. Do not add competitor domains to the client's primary site to inflate coverage.

### Do not confuse organization with access control

SERP SaaS accounts are user-scoped. Groups and Tags organize keywords; they are not client permissions, team seats or a shared client portal. Keep internal labels suitable for exports and only share the files you intend a client to receive.

If your agency needs multi-user approval or client-specific access, plan that process outside this workflow. This guide does not assume collaboration features that the product does not currently provide.

## Build a keyword taxonomy that explains the work

Create Groups around durable reporting questions, then use Tags for cross-cutting attributes. A keyword can have a clear primary organizational home without losing the ability to filter for a campaign or intent.

| Example label         | Reporting question                      | Review note                            |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Group: Core services  | Can people find the main service pages? | Inspect the matched landing pages      |
| Group: Location pages | How do selected local queries perform?  | Preserve the configured search country |
| Tag: Brand            | Is branded visibility stable?           | Report separately from generic phrases |
| Tag: Campaign autumn  | What changed for this selected cohort?  | Keep the cohort stable for comparison  |

Use labels your client will understand. “Priority” should describe an agreed business priority, not a claimed keyword-volume calculation. SERP SaaS does not need a fictional traffic estimate to make an observed ranking useful.

The Group and Tag filters in Ranking Explorer help you review a subset. Check whether your tag selection uses ANY or ALL matching before exporting: those choices can produce different keyword cohorts.

## Choose a sustainable checking cadence

An account with many client Projects needs a capacity plan. Count the keyword sets and environments you will check, estimate the intended frequency and review [Plan limits and scheduling availability](/pricing).

Begin with a manageable routine. A weekly review can support a recurring client update; a temporary, closer watch can help investigate a launch or migration. These are workflow choices, not a guarantee that a particular frequency improves rankings.

Eligible scheduled checks reduce repetitive execution, but they do not replace review. Someone still needs to inspect failed runs, confirm the matched URLs and explain the comparison period. Avoid retrying every missing result as though it were a failed job: a successful Top 10 check can legitimately contain no matching site.

## Compare competitors using the same evidence

Select the primary site and relevant competitors deliberately. In SERP SaaS, competitor analytics uses the stored results from the same checks, with up to five selected competitors. This keeps the comparison tied to a shared observation rather than unrelated searches made at different times.

Explain the metric in the client report. Coverage means the share of keywords in the selected checked dataset where a site was found. It is not market share, traffic share or an estimate of conversions. Position summaries also need their checked-depth and matching context.

Check the denominator: observed coverage uses comparable observations, not every keyword in the Project. An incomplete snapshot without a match is unknown, not confirmed absence, and does not support an out-of-depth conclusion.

> **Reporting note:** A competitor outside the checked Top 10 has an unknown position in that observation. Do not assign position 11 or describe the absence as proof that the competitor has no search visibility.

Review aliases for competitors too. A domain configuration change can affect retrospective matching; document it rather than presenting the resulting change as new competitive movement.

## Create a client-ready report

Start with the Project, environment and dates. State the checked depth and any incomplete observations. Separate meaningful movements from execution problems, then identify a small number of keywords or pages that warrant follow-up.

A useful narrative has three parts:

1. **Observation:** the selected service keywords were found more often in the checked Top 10.
2. **Evidence:** comparable successful checks, a defined period and the affected URLs.
3. **Next step:** inspect the improved pages and verify whether analytics shows a corresponding business benefit.

Use report previews and supported CSV or PDF exports to communicate the dataset. Review the output before forwarding it. When scheduled report delivery is available for the account, its recipient is the account's own verified email; do not assume direct automated delivery to arbitrary client addresses.

You can share a reviewed export with the client through your existing communication process. Avoid including other clients' data, internal-only labels or claims unsupported by the report.

## Make the weekly review repeatable

Use the same checklist each cycle: successful runs, stable keyword cohort, correct environment, current aliases, material changes and a documented next action. Keep a change log for keyword additions so a rising average is not merely the result of removing difficult keywords.

For the investigation part of that checklist, read [how to monitor keyword ranking over time](/blog/how-to-monitor-keyword-ranking). For account sizing, compare the [SERP SaaS Plans](/pricing).

A consistent agency workflow earns its value by making the evidence understandable. Clear boundaries and honest limitations are more useful than a busy dashboard or an unexplained percentage.
